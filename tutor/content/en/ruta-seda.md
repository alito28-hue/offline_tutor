The Silk Road was a vast network of trade routes by land and by sea, opened by China from at least the 1st century BC.

It connected much of Asia with the islands of Southeast Asia, the European Mediterranean and the east coast of Africa.

Its roads began in Chang'an, now Xi'an, then the capital of China, and passed through cities such as Samarkand, Antioch and Constantinople.
