A skeleton is the hard structure that protects the organs of a living thing and gives the body its frame. In mammals, including humans, the skeleton is made of bones. A newborn baby has 300 bones, and an adult has 206 bones because some bones join together.

The skeleton supports the skin and muscles and protects important organs. For example, it protects the brain, the heart and the lungs. The head has the skull and the chest has long curved bones called ribs, joined to the spine at the back.

Skeletons can be inside or outside the body. Mammals, birds, reptiles and fish have an endoskeleton, on the inside. Insects, spiders and crabs have an exoskeleton, on the outside, made of hard plates or shells.
