The separation of powers organizes the state into three functions: making laws, carrying them out and judging according to them. Each one is in the hands of a separate, independent body.

That way, no person or group can concentrate all the power.

The classic explanation comes from Montesquieu. He observed that whoever has power tends to abuse it, and that to prevent this "power must check power".
