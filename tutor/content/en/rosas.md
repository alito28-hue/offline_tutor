Juan Manuel de Rosas was born in Buenos Aires in 1793 and died in England in 1877. He was a rancher, soldier and politician. Between 1835 and 1852 he was the main leader of the Argentine Confederation, in the so-called age of Rosas.

He ruled with total public power and persecuted his opponents, and many of them went into exile.

At the same time, he had great popular support and defended sovereignty against foreign blockades, as at Vuelta de Obligado. He was defeated at the Battle of Caseros in 1852.
