A magnet is made of magnetic metals such as iron, nickel or cobalt. It can pull, or attract, these metals and other magnets. This attraction and pushing away is called magnetism.

All magnets have a north pole and a south pole. Opposite poles attract each other, and the same poles repel each other. For example, north and south attract, while north and north push away.

Magnets have many uses, for example in electric motors. A compass uses the magnetic field of the Earth and points to the North magnetic pole. Some magnets are natural, made of a rock called lodestone or magnetite.
