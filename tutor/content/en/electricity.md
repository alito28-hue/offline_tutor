Electricity is the presence and flow of electric current. It lets us transfer energy to make machines work. Its best-known form is the flow of electrons through copper wires.

Materials that let electricity move through them are called conductors, such as copper wires and other metal items. Plastics are poor conductors and are called insulators, because they stop the electricity.

Electricity can be natural, such as lightning, or made by people, such as in a generator. When electrical charges are not moving, it is called static electricity. When the charges are moving, they are an electric current.
