Christopher Columbus was a navigator in the service of the Crown of Castile. He led the 1492 voyage that brought Europeans to the Americas.

He wanted to reach Asia by sailing west across the Atlantic, and he never fully accepted that he had reached another continent. He first landed on October 12, 1492, on the island of Guanahani, in today's Bahamas.

Many peoples had already lived in the Americas for thousands of years. People speak of a "discovery" because his voyages created a lasting link with Europe, and from then on European powers invaded the continent.

As governor of the island of Hispaniola, he was accused of brutality: the Taino people were divided among the settlers and sold as slaves. He died in Valladolid in 1506.
